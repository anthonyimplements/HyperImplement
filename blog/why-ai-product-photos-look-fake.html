Why AI Product Photos Look Fake (and 7 Fixes I Use)

The tool usually isn't the problem. The inputs are. Here's what makes AI product photos look fake and the seven fixes I use on every product.

You tried an AI tool on your product. The label came out warped, the lighting looked like a video game, and the product itself came out a little different from the real one. So you decided AI product photos just look fake.

Most of the time, they look fake for a few fixable reasons. I'm Anthony. I run HyperImplement out of Chicago and make AI product photos, ads and UGC for e-com brands. Below are the seven fixes I actually use, in the order I use them, plus the exact negative prompt I start from.

Why do AI product photos look fake?

Three things give it away almost every time:

  1. The product changed. The AI didn't keep your product. It redrew it. Logos drift, text turns into gibberish, proportions shift a little. Shoppers can't always say what's wrong, but they feel it.
  2. The light doesn't add up. The product is lit from the left, the shadow falls to the left too, and the room behind it is lit from a window on the right.
  3. Too much is going on. Extra props, extra people, extra shine. Every extra thing is one more spot for the AI to slip.

When I shop for something online, I zoom into the main photo before I buy. If anything looks off in that photo, I don't trust the rest of the page. That's why fake-looking images cost you more than a bad vibe. They cost you trust.

Fix 1: Start from a clean photo on a white background

This is the biggest one. A patio furniture brand I work with sent me their product photos, and to be quite honest, they were messy. The sets were sitting outside on a patio in harsh sun.

So the first thing I did was clean each product onto a plain white background. Once the model has that clean reference, it knows exactly what the product is and what to keep.

What to do today:

  • Take one phone photo of your product in soft daylight, straight on, nothing in the frame but the product.
  • Remove the background so it sits on pure white.
  • Use that file as the reference for every image you generate after it.

If you skip this step, everything below it gets harder.

Fix 2: Give the AI a brand kit

AI loves to "improve" your logo. A brand kit stops that. Mine has the logo, the colors, the typography, the symbol or monogram version of the logo, and a couple of sample headlines.

For the patio brand, I built the brand kit inside Higgsfield and used GPT Image 2 to make sure everything lined up with their real logo. In that video I said it plainly: regardless of the ad you're trying to make, it's crucial to have a brand kit. It's what keeps image number 40 looking like image number 1.

Attach it to every generation, not just the first one.

Fix 3: Tell the model what not to do

Most people only tell the AI what they want. That's half the prompt. The other half is a Negative section that lists what it must not do.

I lean on this in everything I make, even a short film I made with friends. My negatives there were things like no legible text, no distorted background faces and no modern logos. For product photos, I swap in product-specific rules.

The exact negative prompt I start from

Product photo of [product], placed on [surface] in [setting],
soft natural light from the [left/right], matching shadow direction.
Use the attached reference image as the exact product.

Negative:
no changes to the product shape, color, or proportions
no altered, added, or misspelled text or logos
no extra products, props, or hands touching the product
no plastic or overly glossy surfaces
no warped edges or melted details
no mismatched shadows or second light source
no people unless requested

Change the brackets, keep the Negative list, and add a line any time the AI makes a mistake you don't want to see again.

Fix 4: Match the light and shadow

Light is where a lot of "AI look" comes from. If the product sits in a sunny backyard at golden hour, its shadow has to be long and warm and fall away from the sun. If you don't say that, the AI often keeps the flat studio light from your reference photo and pastes it into a scene where it doesn't belong.

So write the light into the prompt:

  • Where the light comes from (left, right, behind).
  • What time of day it is.
  • Which way the shadow falls.

One line like "late afternoon sun from the right, long shadow to the left" fixes more photos than switching tools ever will.

Fix 5: Use fewer AI people (and never as fake customers)

Hands and faces are still where AI slips most often. Fingers bend wrong, grips don't touch the product, and background faces melt. If the photo doesn't need a person, leave the person out.

When you do want a person, keep them in one clear pose and check the hands before anything else. And don't present an AI person as a real customer. The FTC's rule on reviews and testimonials (16 CFR Part 465) bans testimonials from people who don't exist. I wrote more about doing this the right way in How to Make UGC Ads With AI (Without Faking a Review).

Fix 6: Keep real product pixels where they count

Here's how I think about it. Your main image and your detail shots have to show exactly what ships. That's what the shopper zooms into. So those start from real pixels of your real product.

AI is great for the scene around the product: the backyard, the kitchen counter, the seasonal backdrop. It's not great at recreating fine print on a label. If your packaging has small text, ingredients or a detailed logo, keep the product from your real photo and let AI build the world around it.

Fix 7: Rerun, compare, and check it like a shopper

Nobody gets a perfect image on the first try, including me. You're going to have to rerun a couple of them and tweak each prompt. That's normal.

Before any image goes live, I run a quick check:

  1. Product: same shape, color, logo and text as the real thing?
  2. Light: do the light and shadows point the same way?
  3. Edges: any melted corners, extra legs or floating parts?
  4. People: hands holding things correctly, faces not distorted?
  5. Shopper test: if I zoomed in on my phone, would I trust it?

If it fails one, fix the prompt (usually by adding a line to the Negative list) and rerun. Don't post the "almost right" one.

Mistakes that make it worse

  • Switching tools instead of fixing inputs. A new model with the same messy reference gives you the same problems.
  • Generating your main product image from scratch. Start from a real photo instead.
  • Stacking props. Every extra object is another chance for the AI to slip.
  • Posting the first result. A couple of reruns is the cost of a good image.

Questions people ask

Why do AI photos look fake?

Usually because the AI redrew the subject instead of keeping it, the light and shadows don't match, or there's too much in the frame. For product photos, starting from a clean reference on white and adding a Negative section to your prompt fixes most of it.

How do I make AI product photos look less fake?

Start from a real photo of your product on a white background, attach a brand kit, write the light and shadow direction into the prompt, add a list of what the AI must not do, and rerun until it passes a zoom-in check on your phone.

What's the best AI for product photos?

The inputs matter more than the model. I use Higgsfield with GPT Image 2 for stills and Seedance 2.5 for video, but any tool does better with a clean reference, a brand kit and good negatives.

Can AI get the text on my label right?

Sometimes, but I wouldn't count on it for small print. Keep the product from your real photo when the label has to be readable, and let AI handle the scene around it.

The bottom line

AI product photos look fake when the AI has to guess. Give it a clean reference, a brand kit, the light and a list of what not to do, and it stops guessing.
