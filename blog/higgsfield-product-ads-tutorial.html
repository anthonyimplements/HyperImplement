What you need before you open Higgsfield

Two things. If you skip either one, the ads come out wrong no matter how good your prompt is.

  1. A brand kit. Your logo, a symbol or monogram, your colors, your fonts, and a couple of sample headlines.
  2. A clean product photo on a white background. One real photo of the real product, with nothing else in the frame.

That's the big thing. The model can only keep your product and your brand right if you show it what right looks like. In this case, the patio brand's photos were taken outside on a sunny patio. They showed the set, but you'd never use them to sell it. Once I cleaned one onto a white background, the model finally had something it could copy exactly.

Step 1: Build a brand kit the model can follow

I build the brand kit as one image, a single board with everything on it, so I can attach it to every generation. I made mine in Higgsfield with GPT Image 2, but any image tool works for this part.

Put these on the board:

  • Primary logo, plus the symbol and monogram versions
  • Color palette with the hex codes written out
  • Typography: the headline font and the body font
  • Sample headline text, so the model knows how your copy should look
  • Logo usage: where it goes, and what it should never look like

Why bother? Without it, the model guesses. It will invent a logo that's close to yours, or put your website in the wrong font. With the board attached, I could ask for a highway billboard, a spec sheet with the product's measurements, or a cartoon version of the ad, and the logo and colors came out right every time.

Step 2: Clean up the product photo

This step is quick, and it saves you a lot of reruns.

  1. Pick your clearest real photo of the product, the one where the shape and color are most accurate.
  2. Remove the background and put the product on plain white.
  3. Check the edges. Fix any spot where the cutout ate part of the product.
  4. Save it at full resolution and name it something you'll find again.

The white background tells the model exactly where your product starts and stops. Without it, the model tends to blend the product into the old scene, and you get a chair that's a little different from the chair you actually sell. If you want the full version of this step, I broke it down in how to make AI product photos for your Shopify store.

Step 3: Write the prompt (including what NOT to do)

For video I use Seedance 2.5 inside Higgsfield. Attach the white-background photo and the brand kit as references, then write the prompt.

My first prompt for the patio brand was almost boring: a cinematic commercial of the patio set, using the reference image. Simple ads need simple prompts. As the ads got more creative, I moved to a four-part structure:

  1. Shot and action: what the camera sees and what happens
  2. Narration or dialogue: the words, if there are any
  3. Sound: music, ambience, effects
  4. Negatives: everything the model should NOT do

That last part is where most people go wrong. Too often with AI, we tell it what we want and never tell it what to avoid. The negatives are what stop the model from redrawing your product.

Here's a starter template built on that structure. Swap in your own product and scene:

Shot and action: Cinematic commercial. Slow push-in on @product on a sunlit
backyard patio at golden hour. A woman sets a glass of iced tea on the
armrest and sits down. End on a wide shot of the full set.

Narration: none.

Sound: soft acoustic music, birds, the clink of ice in the glass.

Negative: do not change the product's shape, color, material or number of
pieces. No extra furniture or props on the product. No legible text except
the brand logo from @brandkit. No warped hands. No distorted background
faces. No logo placement other than the end card.

Then I end most ads on a simple card with the logo and website from the brand kit, so people know where to click.

Step 4: Pick an ad style (7 that worked for me)

Once the inputs are set, you can make a lot of different ads from the same product. These are the styles I made for the patio brand and when I'd use each one:

Style What happens Use it when
Price-drop sprint A guy sprints past price tags that keep dropping and stops at your product Your price is your edge. The patio brand sold a set around $2,000 in a market where similar sets run around $5,000
Feature close-up The camera stays on one detail, like a wooden armrest One feature sells the product
Same-day delivery "Ordered this morning, delivered today" You deliver fast to a region you can target
Local version The same ad, but it names a city, like Milwaukee You run ads to specific cities
AI UGC-style demo An AI spokesperson shows the product on camera You want the feel of a creator video without hiring one
Comedy skit Something odd happens, and people watch to see what's being sold You need people to stop scrolling
Golden-hour story A short, warm scene of someone using the product You want a calm brand piece

The price-drop sprint is one of my favorites. People see it in their feed and think, "What the heck is this?" and then they watch to the end. The AI UGC-style demo had an amazing click-through rate, and I think part of the reason is that one feature sold it: you can actually set a drink on the armrest. On most patio sets I've owned, the armrest is too thin for that.

One rule on the UGC style: never present an AI person as a real customer giving a real review. That's a fake review, and the FTC's rule on fake reviews applies. I walk through where the line is in how to make UGC ads with AI.

Step 5: Do your research before you generate

Before I make a single ad, I spend a few minutes in the Meta Ad Library. Search your product type, look at what your competitors are running, and pay attention to how long each ad has been live. An ad that has run for months is probably making money.

Then remake the winning idea with your own product and your own spin. Don't copy anyone's ad. Copy the reason it works.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Skipping the white-background photo. This is the number one reason the product comes out different from the real one.
  • Expecting the first generation to be the keeper. You're going to have to rerun a few and tweak each prompt. Budget for that in time and credits.
  • Long, busy scenes. Every extra prop and extra person is one more place for the model to slip. Keep the product as the star.
  • No negatives. Tell the model what not to do, every time.
  • Faking reviews with AI people. It's tempting, and it can get you in real trouble. Label AI spokespeople as what they are.

Common questions

What does Higgsfield cost for product ads?

Higgsfield runs on credits, and each model shows its cost on the Generate button before you run it. For a sense of scale, the 3D-style product ads I made for a golf cart company came out to probably under $10 a clip, versus the thousands a crew and 360 camera rig would cost.

Which Higgsfield model should I use for product video ads?

I use Seedance 2.5 for video. As of November 2026 it makes clips up to 30 seconds and takes up to 50 references, so it can hold your product and brand kit at once. For the brand kit and still images, I used GPT Image 2.

Can Higgsfield make an ad from just my website link?

Higgsfield's Ads Studio can build static ads from a website link, and as of November 9, 2026 it can make video ads from a chat too. I haven't tested the new video version on a client yet. For now I still build the brand kit and prompt by hand, because I want control over every frame.

Do I need to be good at video editing?

No. Every ad above came out of the generator as a finished clip. Basic trimming helps, but you don't need editing skills to make your first one.

Wrap-up

Get the two inputs right, a brand kit and a clean product photo on white, and the rest of the workflow gets a lot easier. Then write simple prompts, tell the model what not to do, and test a few different ad styles to see what your customers respond to.
